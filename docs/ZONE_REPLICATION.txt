EMBERWILD · ZONE OWNERSHIP AND REPLICATION
5 November 2026

Current contract

The shared simulation now has three ownership regions: Cinderwood, Alderhaven,
and the Reach–Alderhaven arrival overlap. Every player, NPC and enemy belongs to
exactly one region at its current position. A transition actor is not copied into
two simulations. The existing character identity, movement sequence, world clock,
combat ownership and persistence remain continuous while walking across the road.

Observer                    Subscriptions
Cinderwood core             Cinderwood + arrival overlap
Alderhaven core             Alderhaven + arrival overlap
Arrival, nearer Cinderwood  Cinderwood + arrival overlap
Arrival, nearer Alderhaven  Alderhaven + arrival overlap

The arrival ownership strip is x=280..450 across the current playable boundary.
The nearer-side switch is x=365. It spans the playable north/south extent to avoid
an unbuffered corner where somebody could leave the strip and still see an actor
on the other side. Physical passage between areas remains the authored gate road;
the replication strip does not make other zone exits playable.

Actors fade from 60 to 75 metres. Actors excluded by the nearer-core switch are
at least 85 metres from the switch, so the change of subscription happens after
they have faded. The common overlap population retains its IDs and client objects
throughout the crossing. Static terrain, buildings and atlas charts remain visible
independently of live actor updates. World geometry streaming across independent
asset packages is not implemented by this change.

Implementation

shared/zone-regions.js defines ownership, primary map and subscriptions.
server/zone-index.js maintains per-region player, NPC and enemy maps. It also
selects recipients for spatial world events and exposes population counts.
World.prepareSnapshots refreshes membership and builds public actor records once
per outgoing frame. Every connection then receives the actor lists from its own
subscriptions, together with its private self state. Monsters still get personal
loot/trust flags only in that observer's packet. Scoped records contain no other
player's inventory, quests, bank or authentication tokens.

The server's state send loop prepares one frame for all connections. The overlap
membership is deduplicated by single ownership. Join/leave notices, effects and
social events use region routing. Effect scopes are captured when the event is
created, so a subsequent move does not readdress an already queued event. Social
messages retain their additional local-range check. Unlocated world events without
explicit scopes are not broadcast by default. Private notices/quest/loot messages
still go directly to their intended recipient.

Party membership and minimal health/name/level/class status are explicit exceptions
to world visibility. A remote party member can remain on the party frame without
sending their world coordinates or creating a character in the local scene.
Party chat uses actual membership at any distance. General chat uses the player's
primary zone. Say/yell can cross the arrival handoff within their ordinary ranges,
rather than encountering an invisible chat boundary at the road midpoint.

The client creates NPC models only from subscribed snapshots and interpolates
NPC movement alongside remote players. Unsubscribed actors and their labels,
reactions and interpolation history are removed. Reappearing actors start from a
fresh authoritative pose. The overlap does not reset movement prediction or its
acknowledgement epoch. Local player movement remains predicted as before.

Simulation work

Noncombat enemies in regions with no interested players skip their ambient/AI
work. Dead-enemy timers still use world time. Existing combat targets continue
being processed to resolve damage/leashing correctly.

City circuits whose entire route is unobserved can sleep. Their route phase is
advanced analytically, including stop durations, before the first subscribed
snapshot. A circuit touching a subscribed region keeps running, even when the
particular actor is currently on the other side of its route. This is needed for
a gate patrol that will walk into the shared overlap. The active path uses the
normal movement/collision rules; every authored circuit is checked end to end.
Greetings, interactions and purchases hold the NPC while somebody addresses it.
No offscreen fast-forward is applied to an actor already visible to a subscriber.

Infrastructure scope

This is zone ownership, scheduling and replication isolation within ONE server
process. It is not yet independent zone workers, distributed handoff, sharding,
multiple databases or a raised capacity limit. The server still shares a 64-player
cap. Global systems such as party membership and the character store remain global.
Per-region actor maps, subscriptions and routing provide boundaries for a later
worker implementation; that will still need explicit state transfer, failure
recovery and authority fencing before claiming multi-process isolation.

Adding another area

Author its playable bounds and map separately. Add its ownership rule and each
adjacent overlap to the shared region definitions. The overlap must be wider than
the live actor fade distance, and must cover every traversable crossing of that
boundary. Register the new runtime bucket in the zone index. Define observer
subscriptions as one core plus its adjacent overlaps; avoid subscribing a traveler
to every neighboring core. Preserve stable actor IDs through ownership changes.
Extend server scheduling and first-frame materialization before loading new NPC
circuits. Do not use a client-reported zone ID as authority for visibility.

Check both directions and the ends of each overlap, selected targets, corpses,
private loot, party status, speech, reconnects and return travel. A geographically
near actor must not disappear merely because the observer changed subscriptions.
Test both content arriving late and content leaving interest, including temporary
network jitter. Revisit visual range and overlap margins together when either grows.

Verification

Tests cover ownership/subscription matrices, unique actor membership, overlap
continuity, excluded-zone state, event scopes, private party status and local chat.
The real WebSocket test uses five clients in both cores and both halves of the
overlap, watches a continuous walk, checks a moving guard, isolates a city effect,
and verifies region population counts. Browser checks retain the same patrol DOM
object during crossing, validate interpolated gait and grounding, verify absent
remote actors, and buy from a traveling merchant at her live position.

Health responses include region population counts; they do not expose character
IDs or credentials. This is a functional scaling foundation, not a production
load benchmark. Future load tests should measure payload bytes, frame construction,
per-region simulation cost, crossing churn and event fan-out with realistic actors.

Release verification (5 November 2026)

227/227 automated tests passed, including four full class campaigns, all city
circuits, offscreen schedule materialization, door/grade checks, private loot/chat,
party status and real WebSocket overlap walking. The city-life browser workflow
passed actor identity continuity, local NPC interpolation/grounding, excluded-zone
actors, a purchase at the live peddler and the raised city view, with no page or
shader errors. The open zone atlas follows the primary-zone change while walking.

The shared server was saved and restarted with all eight character profiles and
two rosters preserved byte-for-byte immediately after restart. Backup:
data/backups/characters-before-city-life-and-zone-replication-20261105T142916Z.json.
