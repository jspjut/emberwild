EMBERWILD STORY DEVELOPMENT METHOD
Revision 1 · 4 November 2026

Start here

The author background is cinderwood-reach.json. It includes the complete prose
background, fixed history, cast, site arcs, disclosure boundaries, ending contract,
and 36 seeded future threads. It is repository game data, not a player document.
shared/story-content.js contains public quest scenes and character responses.
shared/readables.js contains public, attributed documents and their world positions.
The server never serves game-data/story through a public static route.

This method is a recommendation for future chapters. It describes a repeatable
writing process, not new game mechanics or a requirement to use an LLM.

1. Choose a place-sized problem and a real ending

Write one sentence each for: what the player first notices, what is actually
causing it, who wants incompatible things, what the player can change here, and
what will be true when this chapter is finished. Decide the ending before seeding
continuations. At least one important local danger, one relationship, and one
practical need should resolve inside the zone. Do not undo those resolutions in
the next zone merely to make its villain seem important.

The Reach's observable problem is displaced creatures and failing shelters. Its
cause is a retained emergency order spreading through replacement parts. Its
ending is a limited local release supported by six witnesses, food and water.
Alderhaven's paperwork is a next question, not a hostage holding that ending open.

2. Separate facts, accounts, and unchosen possibilities

Maintain three explicit categories. Author truth is fixed continuity. A witness
account belongs to a particular person who saw a limited part. Reserved questions
have no committed answer yet. A future writer may choose among reserved answers;
they may not quietly replace a fixed fact or turn a witness's uncertainty into
omniscience. If a fact must change, record why, affected quests/documents, and the
in-world explanation. Keep dates relative to a named event as well as to the player
chapter so a longer development schedule does not make everyone age accidentally.

The exact intent behind batch seven remains reserved. The fact that Renn survived
the northern road and reached Willowcross does not. Do not later make her a corpse
at that roadstone without explicitly reconciling the established evidence.

3. Give every important person a want and an inconvenient habit

Record want, blind spot, relationship, voice, evidence they can actually know,
and a change visible by the end. Give minor residents ordinary work and opinions.
A playable race is not a single mind or a moral alignment. Show at least two people
who disagree within a tradition. Introduce culture through a task or relationship
before asking a player to remember terminology. Keep room for class differences
without splitting the shared facts into four incompatible histories.

For quest writing, read the dialogue without the speaker's name. If it could be
handed to any representative unchanged, give it a material observation, personal
stake, or limitation of knowledge specific to that person.

4. Outline an evidence route before writing errands

Use: immediate need -> observed contrast -> provisional explanation -> complicating
account -> action -> returned evidence -> changed relationship. Put the route on
the actual world map. Make the first hour's explanations small. Offer one to three
main tasks at a hub; a breadcrumb should explain why the next person can help.
Use later callbacks to ordinary tutorial actions: a greeting can prepare an idea
about consent without starting with a lecture about the charter.

For each beat, fill these fields:
  Quest IDs and prerequisites
  Starting question / what the player currently believes
  Observed thing and actual coordinates / creature / prop
  Speaker and source of their knowledge
  Player action supported by existing mechanics
  New information / what remains uncertain
  Local payoff and changed response
  Optional reading IDs and future-thread IDs
  XP, rewards, travel and class implications

5. Match prose to mechanics and geography

If a quest says pool, show water. If it says behind the inn, verify orientation
and the entrance. A transcription is not a physical memory seed. A memorial name
on a construct does not mean the construct is a dead citizen. A kill objective
must not claim the player negotiated a truce, saved every animal, or permanently
removed shared respawning enemies. Separate a personal story conclusion from a
shared-world transformation until the game implements the latter.

Preserve readable clues outside combat choke points. Validate a walkable approach,
interaction range, collision/line of sight, hover, keyboard access, and readability
at desktop and smaller window sizes. Author coordinates through shared layout
helpers; do not scatter untransformed legacy positions into a later world revision.

6. Give repeated quest structures different dramatic jobs

A reusable intro/collect/fight/entrance/boss/return structure is useful. It cannot
be the story by itself. Decide what the collected object proves, why these enemies
are here, what the entrance reveals, what defeating the keeper enables, and which
belief changes in the return conversation. Each hub needs its own local resolution.

Not every zone needs the Reach's failing-machine plot. A future chapter might be
a disputed festival, migration, inheritance, expedition, or rebuilding project.
Keep the evidence-and-payoff method; vary the type of conflict and kinds of action.
Where a new choice, rescue, escort or diplomatic solution matters, implement and
test that mechanic before writing text that promises it.

7. Put detail in layers, with authors and limits

Quest offers carry motivation and clear action, usually in 45–90 words. Hand-ins
carry a discovery or consequence, usually in 25–70. These are targets, not hard
limits. Readers should get more texture, another viewpoint, or a useful question,
not the missing half of an otherwise unintelligible required story. Aim for short
pages of 70–150 words. Identify a writer or compiler and why this text exists in
this location. A shop invoice, corrected tally, and unfinished letter should not
all sound like the same omniscient history book.

Use a small memorable vocabulary per act. Introduce an unusual term in the same
sentence as its meaning. Treat documents as testimony, not automatic canon dumps.
Keep the internal story bible, future plot plans, and unearned ending pages outside
the public content bundle or gated from normal play as appropriate.

8. Seed continuations as evidence, not advertisements

Give a hook an ID, current source, question, likely future region, possible payoff,
continuity constraints, and status. Make the present clue concrete: a mismatched
seal, a voluntary departure knot, an uncredited shift, an absent map label. The
future can remain undecided. Do not promise rewards, a specific future feature,
or imminent zone access merely to make a mystery enticing.

Distinguish personal, ecological, civic, craft, historical and expedition threads.
Some should resolve gently, some cross zones, and some remain texture. A later
chapter should pick a manageable handful, show how they changed since first seen,
and pay at least one off. Update the registry to adopted, developed, resolved or
retired; name the new quest/source that carries it. Keep other hooks quiet rather
than forcing every local object to belong to one continental conspiracy.

9. Budget progression independently of exposition

First preserve known playable IDs, rewards, class restrictions, objective types
and coordinates. Change their prose and gates intentionally. Audit the dependency
graph for cycles and unavailable level gates. Run all class campaigns after a
story pass. A document should not grant repeat XP or fill a bag merely because it
is interesting. New reward-bearing quests need an XP-budget review, not an assumed
exception for story content. Retain the current common-to-uncommon starter gear
pace and roughly level-10 full-zone finish.

For save compatibility, preserve accepted/completed quest records. Stronger offer
gates may apply to new acceptance; never silently delete a player's accepted quest.
Document any changed gate, including the Reach finale's existing harvest report and the clean-amber
breadcrumb following the actual sap finding.

10. Review the experience, not just the prose

Review in four passes:
  Continuity: dates, identities, source knowledge, cause/effect, lore boundaries.
  Play: routes, enemies, real objective credit, each class, old saves, multiplayer.
  Delivery: visible books, cursor, click/F, pages, Escape, movement transparency,
            no duplicate rewards, distant or wall-blocked reads rejected.
  Emotion: can a player explain what changed for one named person at each hub,
           what they personally accomplished, and why they might take the next road?

Automated campaigns prove reachability and reward totals. Their teleporting between
objectives cannot establish walking pace, emotional clarity or human interest.
After browser verification, a human playthrough should read only required dialogue
first. A second pass can follow optional documents and note which questions arise
naturally. Revise repeated explanations and unsupported promises before adding more
proper nouns or another distant villain.

Done means a complete local arc, valid mechanical actions, a clear consequence,
working readable delivery, consistent player/author knowledge, tested progression,
and a small set of next questions the player has a reason to care about. The Reach
keeps 36 potential threads in reserve; a normal next zone should select a few, not
try to advance all 36 at once.
